One idea for the whole lab: build a local model of the cost at the current point, then move to the model's minimum. The model must fit the structure of the problem.

Slope and curvature

Choose \(B_k\). \(B_k = I/\alpha\) is gradient descent, \(B_k = H\) is Newton's method, \(B_k = J^{\top}J\) is Gauss–Newton for inverse kinematics.

Noise and corners

The slope \(g_k\) is noisy, or the cost has a corner. Noise lets the arm escape and settle; the corner \(\lambda\lVert x\rVert_1\) is kept as it is and keeps joints still.

Beyond vectors

\(\mathcal{X}\) is not flat. The unknown is a whole path of the hand, or a direction on a sphere, or a rotation.

Section 0 · Opening

One arm, one target, four methods

Only the shoulder and the elbow move, so each pose is a point \(q\) on the landscape of \(f(q) = \tfrac12\lVert p(q) - t\rVert^2\), half the squared distance from the tool to the target.
