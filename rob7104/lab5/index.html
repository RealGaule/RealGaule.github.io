What the robot knows about where it is, is a distribution. A filter keeps a shorthand of it: a mean and a covariance, a few sigma points, or a cloud of particles.

Part 1 · Linear

The shorthand is exact. Linear model, Gaussian noise: the belief stays Gaussian, and the Kalman filter's mean and covariance lose nothing.

Part 2 · The stochastic cart

The prediction bends. Noisy wheels turn heading errors into a banana-shaped cloud. In exponential coordinates it is close to a Gaussian again.

Part 3 · Nonlinear filters

Carrying a Gaussian through a curve. Linearise at the mean (EKF) or let sigma points travel (UKF); when the posterior is not Gaussian, use particles.

Lab 3 was the prediction alone (clouds and Fokker–Planck); Lab 5 adds readings. A position reading can estimate velocity because the system is observable (Lab 4).

Section 0 · Opening

Where is the cart?

Every cart gets the same command, "drive 1 m straight ahead"; only the noise in the wheels differs.

Part 1 · Linear

The Kalman filter

A cart on a straight track with position readings only: \(x = (p, v)\), \(x_{k+1} = F x_k + w_k\), \(z_k = H x_k + n_k\).

Part 2 · The stochastic cart

The banana distribution

A differential-drive cart whose wheel speeds carry white noise of strength \(D\); \(DT\) sets how far the cloud spreads.

Part 3 · Nonlinear filters

EKF, UKF, and beyond a Gaussian

The same Kalman formulas, with the cart's nonlinear model in place of \(F\) and \(H\). EKF yellow, UKF orange, particles blue.
